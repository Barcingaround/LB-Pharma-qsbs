## 4. §1045 Rollover Mechanics, Requirements & Risks

### **What is §1045?**
IRC §1045 allows investors to **defer** gain on QSBS by rolling proceeds into replacement QSBS within 60 days.

### **Requirements (ALL must be met)**:
1. ✓ Original stock is QSBS (Series B qualifies)
2. ✓ Held original QSBS > 6 months (May 2022 → March 2026 = ~46 months)
3. ✓ Replacement stock purchased within 60 days of sale
4. ✓ Replacement stock is QSBS of a qualified small business (Evecxia assumption: YES)

### **Mechanics**:
- **Gain deferred**: The gain on LBRX sale is NOT taxed immediately
- **Basis adjustment**: Basis in replacement stock (Evecxia) is REDUCED by the deferred gain
- **Holding period tacks**: Evecxia's QSBS holding period includes the original Series B holding period
  - Series B purchased: May 3, 2022
  - Evecxia purchased: ~April 11, 2026 (within 60 days of lock-up)
  - Evecxia sold: October 15, 2027 (Phase 2 readout)
  - **Tacked holding**: May 3, 2022 → Oct 15, 2027 = 1,991 days (5.45 years) → **§1202 AVAILABLE**

### **Risks**:
1. **Evecxia binary risk**: Phase 2 OCD trial could fail
2. **Liquidity risk**: Evecxia may not be liquid if not public by readout
3. **QSBS eligibility risk**: Evecxia must remain QSBS-eligible (< $50M gross assets at time of investment)
4. **State tax non-conformity**: Some states (e.g., California) do NOT conform to §1202
5. **60-day window risk**: Must find and close Evecxia investment quickly

**IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER**: §1045 rollover execution requires CPA/tax attorney guidance. This model is illustrative only.

## 9. Key Tax Rules Embedded in the Model

1. **Dilution is not a taxable event**: Tax liability is based solely on sale proceeds minus cost basis, calculated lot-by-lot. An investor who paid $100K for Series B and whose shares are now worth ~$100K after dilution has $0 gain and $0 tax.

2. **§1202 requires 5 full years**: Series B issued May 3, 2022 → 5-year mark = May 3, 2027. A sale on March 12, 2026 (lock-up) = 3.86 years → NO §1202. A sale in October 2027 (Phase 3 readout) > 5 years → §1202 IS available.

3. **Tranches are separate tax lots**: Series B (QSBS) and Series C (non-QSBS) do NOT blend. Each has its own purchase date, cost basis, holding period, and QSBS status. Gains and losses calculated independently per lot.

4. **IPO conversion does not erase lot identity**: When preferred converts to common at IPO, the tax lot retains its original purchase date and cost basis. Conversion is generally not a taxable event.

5. **§1045 rollover preserves the journey**: If Series B sold and proceeds reinvested in qualifying replacement QSBS within 60 days, gain is deferred, basis is adjusted downward, and holding period tacks. This means Evecxia (replacement) inherits the May 2022 start date → §1202 becomes available after May 2027.

6. **§1202 exclusion limit**: Greater of $10 million or 10× adjusted basis. For a $100K investment, the 10× test = $1M, so the $10M cap governs. **CRITICAL**: For §1045 rollovers, the 10× basis test uses the ORIGINAL QSBS basis ($100K in Series B), not the adjusted basis in replacement stock (Evecxia).

7. **State conformity varies**: California does NOT conform to §1202 (excludes the exclusion from state tax base). New York partially conforms. Check your state's treatment.
